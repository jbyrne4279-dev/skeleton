Rebuild cost assessment explained

What an RCA report covers, how the rebuild figure is worked out, and when you need one.

(001)

What is a rebuild cost assessment?

A rebuild cost assessment works out the total cost of rebuilding your property from the ground up if it were completely destroyed, for example by fire or flood. Insurers call this the reinstatement cost, and it is the figure your buildings insurance sum insured should be based on. It is not the same as the price you paid or what the property would sell for.

Also known as Reinstatement cost assessment RCA report Insurance rebuild valuation

(002)

Rebuild cost and market value are different

Market value includes the land and reflects local demand. Rebuild cost only reflects labour, materials and the way the building is constructed. This is why many homes are insured for the wrong amount: owners use the purchase price or a quick online estimate rather than a proper rebuild cost assessment.

Market value Land Local demand
Rebuild cost Labour Materials Construction
(003)

What is included in an RCA report?

  • Construction costs for the building as it stands today, using current BCIS rebuild rates for your region.
  • Demolition and site clearance to remove the damaged structure before rebuilding starts.
  • Professional fees for architects, surveyors and engineers needed to manage the rebuild.
  • External works such as garages, outbuildings, boundary walls, drives and, for blocks, car parks and communal areas.
  • VAT where it applies, plus any allowance for building regulations and planning requirements.
(004)

When do you need a rebuild cost assessment?

  • When you buy a property or take out a new buildings insurance policy.
  • Before renewal, if your sum insured has not been checked in the last three years.
  • After an extension, loft conversion, refurbishment or change of use.
  • If you are a freeholder, RMC, RTM company or managing agent responsible for insuring a block.
  • For listed, thatched or period buildings, where specialist materials and trades raise the cost.
(005)

What affects the rebuild cost of a property?

The main factors are the gross internal floor area, the type of construction, the quality of the specification, the number of storeys, access to the site and the region of the UK. Listed status, conservation area rules and unusual materials can add significantly to the figure, which is why a RICS-standard assessment is more reliable than a general calculator.

If your sum insured is too low, insurers can apply the average clause and reduce any claim proportionally, even for partial damage. Insured for 60% of the true rebuild cost? You may only receive 60% of your claim. If you over-insure, you pay higher premiums for cover you can never claim.

A rebuild cost assessment should be carried out by a qualified surveyor following RICS guidance. Our assessments are prepared by RICS-regulated surveyors using current BCIS (Building Cost Information Service) rebuild cost data adjusted for your region and specification.

Our reports set out the method, the measurements and the rebuild figure clearly, so you can pass them straight to your insurer or broker to set or check your sum insured.

A desktop assessment uses floor plans, measurements and photographs and suits most standard homes. A site inspection is recommended for blocks of flats, commercial premises, listed buildings and anything with unusual construction or a high value.

RICS recommends a full rebuild cost assessment at least every three years, with the figure index-linked annually in between. You should also reassess after any extension, conversion or major refurbishment.

Usually the freeholder or management company insures the whole building, so the assessment should cover the entire block. We work with freeholders, RMCs, RTM companies and managing agents to provide whole-building reports.

Most residential desktop reports are delivered within 3 to 5 working days. Site inspections are typically booked within a week, with the report following shortly after. Let us know if you have a renewal deadline.
